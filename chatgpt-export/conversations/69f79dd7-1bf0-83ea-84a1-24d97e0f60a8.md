**Assistant:**

An **urn** is simply a container used to hold cremated remains (ashes). It can be made from different materials depending on purpose, tradition, and travel needs.

### Common materials used for urns

**1. Wood**
- Natural and traditional look  
- Easy for airport X-rays (good for flying)  
- Often used for temporary or permanent storage  

**2. Metal (brass, aluminum, stainless steel)**
- Durable and decorative  
- Common in many cultures  
- ⚠️ Not ideal for air travel because security scanners may not see inside clearly  

**3. Ceramic / Clay**
- Handcrafted and traditional  
- Sometimes used in Hindu rituals  
- Can be fragile  

**4. Biodegradable materials**
- Made from paper, salt, sand, or plant fibers  
- Designed to dissolve in water (very suitable for immersion in the entity["river","Ganges River","sacred river in India"])  
- Common choice for Hindu rites  

**5. Stone / Marble**
- Heavy and long-lasting  
- Usually for permanent placement, not travel  

**6. Glass**
- Artistic and decorative  
- Fragile, not ideal for transport  

---

### Best choice for your situation (air travel + Ganges immersion)
If your goal is to carry ashes from Canada to India and immerse them:
- Use a **non-metal urn** (wood or biodegradable)  
- Many families use a **temporary plastic or cardboard container** for travel, then transfer ashes to a biodegradable urn in India  

---

### Important note
The ashes are usually sealed inside a **plastic bag within the urn**, so the outer container is mainly for protection and ritual.
