## 🔍 What to ask / check when a lawyer declines your case

Since you’ve already consulted “a bunch of firms” and many are unwilling to take your case, these are some of the factors they may be considering — and that you should ask about:

- **Merit of claim / standard of care & causation.** In medical malpractice the lawyer must believe the healthcare provider breached the standard of care *and* that the breach caused your injury. These are tough to prove.  
- **Complexity / cost / risk.** Medical-malpractice cases often require medical experts, obtaining detailed records, long litigation. For example, one firm states the fee for malpractice cases may go up to ~40% because of the higher risk and cost. citeturn0search2  
- **Timing / limitation period.** There are legal time limits for starting a claim in Ontario. If too much time has passed, many firms may decline.  
- **Size of potential recovery.** If the damages or injuries are relatively modest, a law firm may decide it’s not economically viable for them on contingency.  
- **Jurisdiction and specialty.** Some firms may not handle medical malpractice (versus more common personal injury cases), or may limit their intake to very serious injury or death cases.

Yes — you **can file a medical malpractice claim yourself** in Ontario, but it’s **complex, time-sensitive, and expensive** (mostly because of expert evidence). However, there are **some parts you can do free**, and I’ll explain how to do it step-by-step so you understand what’s free vs. what isn’t.

---

## 🧾 1. Filing a claim yourself — what’s free vs. not

| Step | Can you do it free? | Explanation |
|------|----------------------|--------------|
| **Get your medical records** | ✅ *Partly free* | You have a legal right to access your records under Ontario’s *Personal Health Information Protection Act (PHIPA)*. Providers can charge a *reasonable fee* (usually \$30–\$100). |
| **Draft a Statement of Claim** | ✅ *Free if you write it yourself* | You can draft your own civil claim — this costs nothing but time and effort. The challenge is writing it correctly. |
| **File it in Superior Court of Justice** | ❌ *Fee applies* | The filing fee is about **\$229** (as of 2025) for a Statement of Claim in Ontario. You can apply for a **fee waiver** (Form 13A) if you have low income. |
| **Serve it on defendants (doctor/hospital)** | ✅ *Mostly free* | You can personally serve documents or have a friend (18+) do it. A process server costs \$50–\$100. |
| **Expert medical report** | ❌ *Not free — mandatory for malpractice* | You *must* have an expert (doctor/specialist) willing to state that the standard of care was breached. This usually costs **\$2,000–\$10,000**. Courts often dismiss malpractice cases without expert evidence. |
| **Court attendance / case conferences** | ✅ *Free (other than travel)* | You can represent yourself (“self-represented litigant”) at all stages. |
| **Legal help for forms or guidance** | ✅ *Free options exist* | See below for free resources (Law Help Ontario, Pro Bono Ontario, LAO, etc.). |

## 🕓 3. Time limits (don’t miss this)
- You normally have **2 years** from the date you **knew or should have known** that the malpractice occurred.
- There’s also a **15-year ultimate limit** from the date of the incident (even if you didn’t know earlier).
- Courts rarely extend these limits unless there’s a clear reason (like incapacity).

---

## 🧠 4. Realistic self-filing strategy

If you want to move forward yourself, the **bare-minimum plan** is:

1. **Get all your medical records** (hospital, surgeon, family doctor).  
2. **Write a short summary** of events: what happened, when, who treated you, what the harm was.  
3. **Get at least one medical opinion** (maybe a retired or independent doctor).  
4. **Draft your Statement of Claim** using Ontario’s [Rule 25 Form 14A](https://www.ontariocourts.ca/scj/practice/rules-of-civil-procedure/#rule25).  
5. **File it at the Superior Court of Justice** and serve it on defendants.  
6. **Use Pro Bono Ontario** to check your draft before filing.

## ⚖️ Step 1. Understand what the “fee waiver” does

A **fee waiver** means you **don’t pay court fees** for things like:
- Filing your **Statement of Claim**
- Issuing a **Summons or Motion**
- Filing a **Statement of Defence**
- Copying or certifying court documents
- Setting a matter down for trial  

It **does not** cover costs like:
- Expert witness fees  
- Process servers or mail couriers  
- Lawyer or paralegal fees (if you hire one)

---

## 📝 Step 2. Download or get the correct form

You need to fill out **Form 13A – Request for Fee Waiver**.

### 🗂️ Where to get it:
- Download PDF from the Ontario Courts website:  
  🔗 [https://www.ontario.ca/document/fee-waiver-guide/forms](https://www.ontario.ca/document/fee-waiver-guide/forms)
- Or ask for it in person at any **Superior Court of Justice** or **Small Claims Court** counter.  
  (You can tell the clerk: “I’m filing a Statement of Claim and would like to apply for a fee waiver.”)

---

## 🪶 Step 3. Fill out **Form 13A – Request for Fee Waiver**

It’s a 3-page form. You’ll need:

### **Part A: Your information**
- Your full name, address, and phone/email.
- Type of case (civil, medical malpractice).
- Court location (e.g., Toronto, Brampton, Ottawa, etc.).

### **Part B: Financial information**
You’ll need to confirm:
- You’re **receiving social assistance** (e.g., OW, ODSP)  
  *OR* your **income is below the guideline**.
- You have **limited assets** (no significant savings, home equity, etc.).

**Tip:** You do **not** have to attach your bank statements.  
Instead, just check the appropriate box (e.g., “I receive Ontario Works or ODSP”).

### **Income thresholds (approx., 2025):**
If your **monthly income** is below about:
- \$2,100 (single person)
- \$3,100 (two people)
- \$3,700 (family of three)
…then you’ll likely qualify.

---

## 🧾 Step 4. Submit the waiver and your claim **together**

You can file in one of two ways:

### 🏛️ **In person**
1. Go to your nearest **Superior Court of Justice** (civil counter).  
   Find your court at: [https://www.ontariocourts.ca/scj/locations/](https://www.ontariocourts.ca/scj/locations/)
2. Bring:
   - **Form 13A** (fee waiver request)
   - **Your Statement of Claim**
   - **Photo ID**
3. Hand it to the clerk and say:  
   “I am self-represented, filing a Statement of Claim, and requesting a fee waiver because of low income.”
4. If they approve, they’ll **stamp and issue** your claim immediately without charging fees.

### 💻 **Online (Civil Submissions Portal)**
If the court allows electronic filing:
- Go to [https://www.ontario.ca/page/file-civil-claim-online](https://www.ontario.ca/page/file-civil-claim-online)
- Upload your **Form 13A** *and* your **Statement of Claim**
- In the comment box, type:  
  “Fee waiver requested — please review attached Form 13A.”
- You’ll receive confirmation by email once it’s approved.

---

## ✅ Step 5. Wait for approval (if not instant)

Most clerks approve simple fee waivers **on the spot** (especially if you’re on ODSP or Ontario Works).  
Otherwise, it may take **1–3 business days** for a registrar to approve it.  
You’ll then get a **Fee Waiver Certificate Number** (e.g., FW-123456).  
Keep that number — you’ll use it for all future filings in the same case.

---

## ⚠️ Step 6. Keep this in mind

- The fee waiver only applies **for this case** — if you start another case, you’ll need a new one.  
- If your financial situation changes, you must inform the court.  
- You’ll still need to pay **expert witness or record-copy fees** — these aren’t covered.

## 🧾 **2. What to Include in Your Statement of Claim (Form 14A)**

A strong **Statement of Claim** has three main sections:

### 🧩 **(a) Parties**
- Your full name and address (you = *Plaintiff*).  
- The hospital, doctor(s), or clinic you are suing (*Defendant(s)*).  
- Example:  
  > “The Plaintiff, Amit Puri, resides in Mississauga, Ontario.  
  > The Defendant, Dr. John Smith, is a physician practicing at Trillium Health Partners, 100 Queensway W, Mississauga.”

---

### 🩺 **(b) Facts of the Case**
Explain in **chronological order** what happened, using clear, factual language.  
Include:
1. Date(s) of treatment or surgery  
2. What procedure or diagnosis occurred  
3. What went wrong or was neglected  
4. The resulting harm or damage to your health  

Example:
> On July 15, 2021, the Plaintiff underwent a surgical procedure performed by the Defendant. The Defendant failed to provide adequate post-surgical monitoring and did not respond to reported symptoms of infection. As a result, the Plaintiff suffered complications requiring hospitalization and ongoing disability.

---

### ⚖️ **(c) Remedy or Relief Sought**
State what you are asking the court for:
- **General damages** (for pain and suffering)  
- **Special damages** (medical costs, lost income)  
- **Interest and costs** (if applicable)  

Example:
> The Plaintiff claims:
> 1. General damages in the amount of \$500,000;  
> 2. Special damages to be proven at trial;  
> 3. Pre-judgment and post-judgment interest under the Courts of Justice Act;  
> 4. Such further and other relief as this Honourable Court may deem just.

---

## 🧍‍♂️ **3. Formatting Rules**
- Type or print clearly.  
- Use **8.5 x 11-inch white paper**.  
- Leave a **top margin** for the court stamp.  
- Use **double spacing**.  
- Number the paragraphs.  

---

## 🧠 **4. Filing Steps**

1. **Get your Fee Waiver Certificate** (Form 13A) approved first.  
2. **Prepare 3 copies** of your Statement of Claim (Form 14A).  
3. Go to the **Superior Court of Justice (Civil Office)** (Brampton or Toronto).  
4. Give the clerk:  
   - Your original and 2 copies of Form 14A  
   - Your fee waiver certificate  
   - Your valid ID  
5. The clerk will:  
   - **Stamp** and file your claim  
   - Give you a **court file number**  
   - Return your copies for serving the defendants  

---

## 📬 **5. Next Step After Filing: Service**
Once your claim is issued, you must **serve it** on the defendants within **6 months**, using:
- Personal delivery by someone over 18 (not you), or  
- Registered mail or courier  

Then you complete **Form 4A (Affidavit of Service)** and file it with the court.
