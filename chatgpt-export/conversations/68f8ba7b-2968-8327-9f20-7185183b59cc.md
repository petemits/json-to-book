### **1. Ask for a full, itemized statement**
Write (or email) the **legal service provider and the condo corporation** asking for:
- A **breakdown of all charges** added to the account, including dates, reasons, and who authorized them.  
- **Confirmation of the current balance** as of a specific date (e.g., *“Please confirm the final amount due as of October 22, 2025, so I can make payment immediately.”*).  
- A **copy of any invoices** that justify the additional fees.

If they are acting for your condo corporation, they must provide transparent billing under the **Ontario Condominium Act**, which requires clarity in charges to owners.

---

### **2. Mention your intent to pay**
You should clearly state that you’re not refusing to pay — you just want **clarity before payment**. For example:

> “I have received Form T14 and am willing to make payment, but the amount keeps changing without proper explanation. Please confirm the accurate, final amount due and provide an itemized breakdown so that I can pay promptly.”

This helps protect you if the matter ever goes to the Condominium Authority Tribunal or court — it shows good faith.

---

### **3. Contact the Condo Management Office**
If the legal firm isn’t transparent, copy your **property manager** or **condo board** on your email. State that you are trying to make payment but the legal provider is **adding unexplained charges**, and ask for their **intervention or clarification**.

---

### **4. Check whether they’re allowed to add ongoing legal fees**
In many cases, **additional legal fees** can only be added if:
- You were notified of them in advance, **and**
- They are **reasonable and necessary** to recover the debt.

If you believe they’re overcharging, you can dispute the charges through:
- The **Condominium Authority Tribunal (CAT)** (for chargeback disputes),
- Or the **Law Society of Ontario** (if you believe the lawyer is acting improperly or unethically).

---

### **5. Consider paying the uncontested portion**
If you’re ready to pay, you could pay the **amount you originally agreed to (the T14 balance)** and include a written note or email saying:

> “This payment is made under protest for the confirmed amount in Form T14. Any additional charges added after this date are disputed until clarified.”

That way, you’ve shown willingness to pay while protecting your right to dispute unfair additions.

## 🏛️ What Is the CAO Tribunal (CAT)?
The **Condominium Authority Tribunal (CAT)** is part of the **Condominium Authority of Ontario (CAO)** — a government-established body that helps **condo owners, boards, and managers resolve disputes** **without going to court**.

It’s like a small online court specifically for **condo-related issues**.

---

## ⚖️ What the CAT Does
The **CAT** handles disputes between:
- **Condo owners** (like you)  
- **Condo corporations** (your building’s board or management)  

It provides a **step-by-step online process** to solve problems such as:
- Incorrect or **unreasonable charges** added to your condo account  
- **Unexplained legal fees** or administrative fees  
- **Access to condo records or financial statements**  
- **Unfair actions by the condo board or manager**

---

## 💻 How It Works
Everything is done **online** — you don’t need a lawyer.

### The 3 Stages:
1. **Negotiation** – You and the condo try to settle the issue informally using the CAT’s secure online platform.  
2. **Mediation** – If negotiation fails, a CAT mediator helps both sides reach an agreement.  
3. **Tribunal Decision** – If no agreement, a CAT **Member (like a judge)** reviews the evidence and makes a **binding legal decision**.  

The process is **much cheaper and faster** than court — most fees are under **$50–$200 total**.

---

## 📌 Why You Might Use It
In your case, the CAT can help if:
- The condo’s **lawyer or management** keeps **adding charges** unfairly.  
- You’re being **charged without explanation**.  
- You’re being **harassed or threatened** over payments you’re trying to make.  

---

3. You’ll create an account and fill in:
   - Your name and condo unit number
