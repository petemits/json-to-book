Yes—you’re basically describing a **self-organized homeowners group**, and it *can* work—but there are a few important realities to understand before jumping in.

### What you're thinking of
On a street where everyone owns their own house (no condo corporation or formal HOA), residents can voluntarily create something like:
- a **neighbourhood association**
- a **resident committee**
- or an informal “board”

This group can:
- Coordinate things like security, snow removal, landscaping for shared areas
- Organize events or community decisions
- Pool money for shared improvements (e.g., cameras, lighting)

### The key limitation (this is important)
Unlike a condo board or HOA:
- You **cannot force participation**
- You **cannot enforce rules legally** unless people *voluntarily agree*
- You **cannot collect mandatory fees** without a legal structure

So it’s more about **cooperation than authority**.

### If you want real power (more structured approach)
To make it stronger and more “official,” you could:
1. Create a **formal association** (non-profit style)
2. Draft bylaws (who pays, how decisions are made)
3. Get residents to **sign agreements**

Even then, enforcement is still limited unless it's tied to property titles (which is how formal HOAs work).

### Reality check
Your idea is strong from a **community empowerment perspective**, but:
- It works best when neighbours are cooperative
- It struggles if even a few people refuse to participate
- It cannot replace a legally binding condo/HOA system

### A smarter version of your idea
