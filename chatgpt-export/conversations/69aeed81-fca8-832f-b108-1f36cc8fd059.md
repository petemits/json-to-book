Yes 👍 — you **can make oil from many vegetables**, but there are some important details.

### 1. Vegetables That Naturally Contain Oil
Oil comes from the **fat inside plant cells**. Some plants have a lot of oil, so extracting it is practical.

Common examples:

- **Olives** → entity["food","Olive oil"]  
- **Soybeans** → entity["food","Soybean oil"]  
- **Corn kernels** → entity["food","Corn oil"]  
- **Sunflower seeds** → entity["food","Sunflower oil"]  
- **Canola seeds** → entity["food","Canola oil"]  
- **Peanuts** → entity["food","Peanut oil"]  

These plants contain **15–50% oil**, so pressing them gives a useful amount.

### 2. Vegetables With Very Little Oil
Many common vegetables have **almost no fat**, so you cannot realistically make oil from them.

Examples:
- carrots  
- potatoes  
- cucumbers  
- lettuce  
- cabbage  

They contain **less than 1% oil**, mostly water and carbohydrates.

### 3. How Oil Is Extracted
Industrially there are two main methods:

**1️⃣ Mechanical pressing**
- crush or grind
- apply pressure
- oil flows out  
Example: olive presses.

**2️⃣ Solvent extraction**
- crushed plant soaked in solvent (often hexane)
- solvent dissolves oil
- solvent evaporated → pure oil remains

### 4. Interesting Fact
Even vegetables with tiny oil amounts **technically contain some fat**, but extracting it would require **tons of vegetables** to get a small bottle.

Example:
- 1 ton of carrots might give only **a few hundred grams of oil**.

### 5. What People Often Do Instead
Sometimes people **infuse vegetables into oil** rather than extracting oil from them.

Example:
- garlic oil  
- chili oil  
- herb oil  

The base oil (like olive oil) absorbs flavor.
