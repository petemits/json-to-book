**Assistant:**

That can happen in some condo buildings, especially older ones or buildings that have had partial renovations over the years.

If only one small wall has insulation and the others do not, a few possibilities are:

- **That wall is an exterior wall** facing outdoors, while the others are interior partition walls.
- **A previous repair or renovation** may have added insulation only to that section.
- **Soundproofing** may have been installed in one wall near plumbing, elevators, mechanical rooms, or neighboring units.
- **Fire-stopping or code upgrades** may have required insulation in a specific area but not throughout the unit.
- In some older condo buildings, interior walls are simply metal or wood studs with drywall and little or no insulation.

Since your walls are currently open, this is actually the best time to inspect:
- Type of framing (wood or metal studs)
- Presence of vapor barriers
- Existing insulation type and thickness
- Plumbing, electrical, and HVAC runs
- Any signs of moisture, mold, or air leakage

If you're concerned about comfort, noise, or energy efficiency, you could consider adding:
- Mineral wool (excellent for soundproofing and fire resistance)
- Fiberglass batts
- Acoustic insulation between rooms

A question: when you say the walls are broken again, were they opened because of a repair (such as water damage, plumbing work, bed bug treatment access, electrical work, etc.), or are you doing another renovation? Also, is this wall on the outside of the building or between you and a neighboring condo unit? That detail would help explain why only that section is insulated.

If you're only painting and patching holes, and you've noticed that one exposed section of wall has insulation while the rest doesn't, that's not necessarily unusual.

In many condo units:
