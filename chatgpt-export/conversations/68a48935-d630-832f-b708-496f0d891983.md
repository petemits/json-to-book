Dear Sir/Madam,


I am writing to request your legal assistance in pursuing a civil litigation case concerning medical malpractice and professional negligence involving a finger surgery that was conducted without proper informed consent, adequate diagnostic care, or adherence to established ethical procedures. The surgery has left me with permanent pain, impaired hand function, and psychological distress.


In November 2023, I sustained a finger dislocation due to the fall in my bathroom, I went to the walkin clinic, the attending doctor performed Xray and referred me to present the injury at Trillium Hospital in Mississauga. The first attempt to relocate my finger involved twisting it around in an effort to return it to its original position. This attempt failed. The emergency doctor attending to me was not a surgeon, but a physician whose role was limited to referring me to a specialist. He referred me to a finger plastic surgeon named Dr. Paul Walker in Burlington St. Joseph Hospital, implying that most surgeries of this kind result in full finger function. However, he could not, or did not, explain the severity of my specific case or the potential complications that might arise from the surgery.


The surgeon then strongly urged me to undergo the procedure, explaining that it would involve cutting the finger to place it back into its original location. I was assured that the surgery would be straightforward, pain-free, and fully successful. Based on these representations, I proceeded but at no point was I provided with a written or verbal consent form, nor was I informed of the risks, long-term outcomes, or alternative treatments.


Under standard medical guidelines, obtaining a patient’s informed consent is a mandatory legal and ethical responsibility, particularly for surgical procedures. This includes explaining the risks, possible results, and all other available treatment options. I now understand that this surgery should never have been performed without a signed consent form and a thorough explanation that I clearly understood.


Furthermore, the doctors were aware I had presented for treatment two weeks after my injury, yet they insisted on immediate surgery, claiming urgency. They told me I should have come earlier, but still pressed for the operation — even though, according to later medical consultations, the outcome would not have changed had the surgery been performed months later..During surgery, the surgeon remarked I should have come in earlier but still assured me of success. After the operation, he said I would regain full finger function in 6–8 months and referred me to physiotherapy. The physiotherapist discovered a hanging stitch, stating therapy could not proceed until it was removed and that no improvement was possible otherwise. Dissolving stitches should have been used. My family doctor later advised that regaining full function would require breaking the finger again. A specialist and another surgeon have since confirmed I should have been informed of the likely outcome and that the performing surgeon should have been more transparent. 


No MRI was ordered, and no physiotherapy was recommended as a non-surgical alternative. I firmly believe that if a physiotherapist had been consulted, I could have pursued functional recovery without invasive surgery. It is the responsibility of medical professionals to refer patients to the most appropriate care — a responsibility that was neglected.


I also wish to emphasize that I have lived with schizophrenia for over 20 years and suffered a brain stroke in 2021, which impaired my memory. These are documented medical conditions that the doctors should have reviewed before making surgical decisions. Given my medical background, it was their duty to ensure that I fully understood the implications before proceeding. Had the risks been made clear, I would have refused surgery.


In my past, I have taken responsibility for my actions — for example, after being involved in a car accident that was partially my fault, I chose not to make an insurance claim. I mention this to show that I act fairly and responsibly. In this case, however, the decision to operate and the eventual outcome rested entirely on the surgeon’s professional judgment. If a surgeon proceeds knowing the surgery may fail, without disclosing this and without documented consent, the liability is entirely theirs.


Since the surgery, I have suffered chronic pain, reduced hand function, and significant emotional strain. My ability to work — after a 26-year career in sales and customer service — has been compromised. I have filed complaints with my family doctor and medical regulators, but I now believe legal action is the only appropriate step toward justice.


I would be grateful for your guidance in initiating civil proceedings. Please advise what documents or evidence you require. I am prepared to provide medical records, statements, and all relevant materials to support my claim.


Thank you for your time, understanding, and commitment.


Sincerely,

Amit Puri
