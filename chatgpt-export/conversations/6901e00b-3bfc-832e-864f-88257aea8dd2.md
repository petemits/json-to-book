**Assistant:**

Short answer: yes — FRP (Factory Reset Protection) is tied to the Google account on the phone (and Samsung devices can also use a Samsung account). To make FRP work for your Samsung, add a Google account (and optionally a Samsung account), keep it on the device, and make sure device protection features are enabled. Below are clear steps and a few helpful tips.

# Add a Google account (One UI / Samsung)
1. Open **Settings**.  
2. Tap **Accounts and backup** (on older phones: **Accounts** or **Cloud and accounts**).  
3. Tap **Accounts**.  
4. Tap **Add account** → **Google**.  
5. Sign in with the Google email (Gmail) address and password you want tied to the phone. Complete any on-screen verification (2-step verification if enabled).  
6. After sign-in, make sure **Sync** is on for the account.

# Add (or check) Samsung account — optional but useful
1. Settings → **Accounts and backup** → **Accounts** → **Add account** → **Samsung account**.  
2. Sign in / create an account.  
3. Go to **Settings** → **Biometrics and security** → **Find My Mobile** and enable it (this can help recover/unlock the phone via Samsung’s service later).

# Confirm device protection is active
- Settings → **Security** (or **Lock screen** / **Biometrics and security**) → confirm you have a screen lock set (PIN, pattern or password) — FRP works best when a secure lock is active.  
- Settings → **Security** → **Find My Device** (Google) — enable it so Google services can locate/verify the device after a reset.  
- Make sure the phone has internet access (Wi-Fi or mobile data) when you sign in so the account is fully registered.

# Important notes / troubleshooting
- **FRP purpose:** If someone performs a factory reset and tries to set up the phone, Android will ask for the Google account that was previously on the phone. That’s FRP. So *to have FRP protect your phone in future*, **add the Google account now and do not remove it before a reset**.  
- **If the phone is already FRP-locked:** the only legitimate way to unlock it is to sign in with the Google account (email + password) that was previously used on the device, or to unlock through Samsung’s Find My Mobile if a Samsung account was active. I can’t help bypass FRP.  
- **Selling or giving away the phone:** Before handing it off, **remove the Google and Samsung accounts** (Settings → Accounts → select account → Remove account) and then perform a factory reset so the new owner won’t be FRP-locked.  
- **Account recovery:** If you forget the Google account or password, use Google’s account recovery (accounts.google.com) to regain access — this is the correct route if you’re the owner.  
- **Two-factor auth:** If your Google account uses 2FA, make sure you have access to the second factor when signing in (phone, authenticator app, backup codes).

# Two scenarios & what to do

**A. You can use the phone right now (you’re inside Android / Settings)**  
If the phone is unlocked and you can reach Settings, you *can* make it yours:

1. Settings → **Accounts and backup** → **Accounts** → **Add account** → **Google** — sign in with your Google account.  
2. (Optional) Add a Samsung account the same way (Add account → Samsung account).  
3. Remove the donor’s accounts: Settings → Accounts → select the old Google and Samsung accounts → **Remove account**.  
4. Now do a clean factory reset: Settings → **General management** (or **System**) → **Reset** → **Factory data reset** → **Reset**.  
5. After the reset, when setup runs, sign in with *your* Google account — FRP will now be tied to your account.

**B. The phone is FRP-locked and at the setup screen (asks for previous owner’s Google account)**  
You cannot add a new account to bypass this. Legitimate options:

- **Ask the donor to remove the device from their Google account remotely** (fastest). They can sign into their Google account on a computer/phone, go to their account → Devices or Device activity, find the device, and remove/sign out that device. After they remove it, wait a few minutes and reboot the phone — then the setup may proceed without asking for the old credentials.

- **Ask the donor to remove the Samsung account** (if there is one) by signing into Samsung’s account portal (Find My Mobile) and removing the device.

- **If the donor can’t do that** (lost account, unreachable), contact Samsung support and explain the situation. They may require proof the device was legally transferred to you (donation paperwork, a signed note from the donor, ID, etc.). Policies vary by country/carrier and Samsung will decide whether they can help. Be prepared that they may refuse without solid proof of legal transfer.
