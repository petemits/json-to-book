## **1. Mechanical Sound Waves**

## **2. Microphone – Mechanical → Electrical**

A **microphone** converts these air vibrations into an **analog voltage signal**:

### **A. Dynamic Microphone (Moving Coil)**

1. Sound waves push a **diaphragm**.  
2. Diaphragm moves a **coil of wire** inside a **magnetic field**.  
3. Motion induces a **voltage** in the coil proportional to the sound wave.  

**Result:** An **analog electrical signal** representing the original sound wave.

---

### **B. Condenser Microphone (Capacitive)**

1. Diaphragm is part of a **capacitor**.  
2. Sound waves make the diaphragm move, changing **capacitance**.  
3. This variation is converted into a **voltage signal** using a small internal amplifier.  

**Result:** High-fidelity analog signal that mirrors the mechanical sound wave.

---

### **C. Piezoelectric Microphone**

1. Diaphragm presses on a **piezoelectric material**.  
2. Mechanical stress generates a **voltage**.  

---

## **3. Output – Analog Signal**

- The output voltage **varies continuously** with the air pressure (sound wave).  
- This signal can now be processed by:  
  - **Amplifiers**  
  - **Analog filters**  
  - **Analog-to-Digital Converters (ADC)** for digital processing

**Example:**  
- A 1 kHz tone vibrating in the air → Microphone outputs a 1 kHz sine wave voltage, with amplitude proportional to loudness.

3. **Outputs anti-noise** as analog voltage to speakers or headphones.  
4. Air vibrations from the speakers **interfere with the original sound**, reducing what you hear.
