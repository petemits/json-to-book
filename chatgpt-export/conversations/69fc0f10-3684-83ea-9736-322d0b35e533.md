In Hindu traditions, the “final rites” after a person’s death are commonly called **Antyesti** (last sacrifice) or **Antim Sanskar** (final sacrament). These rituals help honor the deceased, support the family through mourning, and spiritually guide the soul’s journey.

The exact customs vary by region, language, caste traditions, and family practices, but the main elements often include:

1. **Preparation of the body**
   - The body is washed and dressed in clean clothes.
   - Prayers, mantras, flowers, sandalwood paste, or holy water may be used.
   - In many traditions, the body is placed with the head facing south.

2. **Cremation**
   - Hindus traditionally cremate the body, usually within 24 hours if possible.
   - A close family member, often the eldest son or nearest relative, performs key rites.
   - Sacred prayers from texts like the *Garuda Purana* or *Bhagavad Gita* may be recited.
   - The ritual fire symbolizes release of the soul from the physical body.

3. **Asthi Visarjan (immersion of ashes)**
   - Ashes and remaining bone fragments are collected afterward.
   - They are often immersed in a sacred river, especially the entity["place","Ganges River","India"], which is considered spiritually purifying.
   - Families outside India may use local rivers, lakes, or oceans according to local law and custom.

4. **Mourning period**
   - A mourning period commonly lasts 10–13 days.
   - Family members may avoid celebrations and perform daily prayers.

5. **Shraddha and Pind Daan**
   - Ritual offerings of food, water, and prayers are made for the departed soul and ancestors.
   - These ceremonies are believed to help the soul attain peace and continue its spiritual journey.

Many Hindus believe the soul (Atman) continues after death through reincarnation until attaining **moksha** (liberation).
