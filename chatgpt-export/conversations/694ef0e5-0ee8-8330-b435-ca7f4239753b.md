---

## Who gives condo board members their power?

**Condo board members do NOT choose their own powers.**  
They are **mandated and limited by the condominium corporation**, which itself is governed by law.

Their authority comes from **4 sources only**:

### 1️⃣ The **Condominium Act, 1998** (the law)
This is the **highest authority**.  
Board members **cannot act outside this Act**, even if they want to.

If they do → their actions can be challenged and overturned.

---

### 2️⃣ The Corporation’s **Declaration**
This document:
- Defines **unit use**
- Defines **owner rights**
- Defines **what the board can and cannot regulate**

👉 If something is **not restricted in the declaration**, the board **cannot invent a restriction**.

---

### 3️⃣ The Corporation’s **By-laws**
By-laws cover:
- How directors are elected
- How meetings run
- Officer roles (president, treasurer, etc.)

By-laws **cannot override the Act or the Declaration**.

---

### 4️⃣ **Rules** (lowest level of power)
Rules can only:
- Promote **safety**
- Promote **security**
- Protect **shared property**

❗ Rules **CANNOT**:
- Take away ownership rights
- Create penalties
- Act retroactively
- Conflict with the declaration or Act

---

## How do board members get their position?

### 🔹 They are **ELECTED by unit owners**
- Usually at the AGM
- Owners vote
- Board members are **not appointed permanently**
- They are **replaceable**

They are **fiduciaries**, meaning:
> They must act **honestly, in good faith, and in the best interests of the corporation**, not personally, emotionally, or selectively.

---

## Can board members “decide” things on their own?

### ❌ NO — not personally
Board members:
- Cannot enforce **personal opinions**
- Cannot selectively target owners
- Cannot create new obligations without authority
- Cannot levy **fines** (this is illegal in Ontario)

They must act:
- By **formal board resolution**
- Within **documented authority**
- With **equal treatment of all owners**

---

## What they **cannot** do (very important)

❌ Issue fines or penalties  
❌ Enforce rules that don’t exist  
❌ Change owner rights without a declaration amendment  
❌ Act without notice or due process  
❌ Ignore owner requests for records  
❌ Apply rules inconsistently  

---

## Who actually controls the board?

### ✅ The **owners**
Owners can:
- Requisition meetings
- Vote directors out
- Demand records
- Challenge actions at the **Condominium Authority Tribunal (CAT)** or court

---

## Simple summary

> **Board members do not choose their powers.  
> Their powers are mandated, restricted, and controlled by the Condominium Act, the Declaration, and the owners.**

## Do condo board members get paid?

- They serve **without remuneration**

---

## When *can* they be paid? (rare)

### ✅ **ONLY if the Declaration or By-laws explicitly allow it**
Payment is allowed **only if**:
- The condo **Declaration or By-laws clearly authorize compensation**, **and**
- Owners approved those documents

Most condos **do not** allow this.

---

## What *is* allowed?

### ✔ Reimbursement of expenses
Board members **can be reimbursed** for:
- Legitimate, documented expenses
- Incurred while acting for the corporation  
Examples:
- Postage
- Printing
- Travel for condo business

❗This is **not salary** and **not profit**

---

## What is NOT allowed?

❌ Secret payments  
❌ “Honorariums” without authority  
❌ Free condo fees  
❌ Gift cards or perks paid from condo funds  
❌ Management company kickbacks  

All of the above can be:
- **Breach of fiduciary duty**
- **Misuse of condo funds**
- **CAT or court violations**

### Short answer  
**A condo board does NOT personally hire contractors.**  
**The condominium corporation hires contractors**, and the board only acts **as an agent of the corporation**, within strict limits.

---

## Who is legally allowed to hire a contractor?

### ✅ The **Condominium Corporation**
- The corporation is the legal entity
- Contracts are **between the corporation and the contractor**
- **Not** between an individual board member and a contractor

Board members **cannot** hire contractors in their own name.

---

## How does the board hire a contractor properly?

The board must:

1️⃣ Act **by board resolution** (meeting + minutes)  
2️⃣ Stay within the **approved budget**  
3️⃣ Follow the **Act, Declaration, and By-laws**  
4️⃣ Use **management company procedures** (if one exists)  
5️⃣ Avoid **conflicts of interest**

If these steps are skipped → the contract can be challenged.

---

## Role of the Property Manager

If the condo has a property manager:
- The **manager usually arranges contractors**
- The board **authorizes**, not personally selects
- The manager signs or facilitates contracts **for the corporation**

---

## Emergency vs Non-Emergency

### 🚨 Emergency repairs
- Board or manager can act quickly
- Must be **necessary to prevent damage or danger**
- Still must report it afterward to the board

### 🛠️ Non-emergency repairs
- Requires approval
- Often requires **multiple quotes**
- Must be budgeted or owner-approved if large

---

## What board members CANNOT do

❌ Hire friends or relatives without disclosure  
❌ Bypass the corporation or manager  
❌ Spend outside approved authority  
❌ Order work on **private units** unless authorized  
❌ Approve work to “punish” or target an owner  

---

## Very important (owner rights)

If the work:
- Affects **your unit**
- Is charged back to you
- Is not clearly authorized in the Declaration

👉 You can demand:
- Proof of authority
- Board resolution
- Copy of the contract
- Invoices and scope of work

Yes — as an owner, you **absolutely have the right to know how the condominium corporation is spending money**. This is a key protection under the **Condominium Act, 1998 (Ontario)**.  

Here’s the breakdown:

---

## 1️⃣ What owners can see

### Financial statements
- Annual **audited financial statements** (or reviewed statements for smaller condos)
- Shows **revenues, expenses, and reserve fund usage**

### Budgets
- **Proposed annual budget** before approval at AGM
- Owners can review and **vote on it** in some cases

### Ledgers and invoices
- Owners can request:
  - **Detailed general ledger**
  - **Invoices for contractors and services**
  - **Receipts for reimbursements**

### Meeting minutes
- Board meeting minutes (usually **excluding confidential personal info**)  
- Shows **decisions on spending**  

---

## 2️⃣ Legal basis

Under the **Condominium Act, 1998**:

> Owners have the right to **inspect the books and records** of the corporation.  
> This includes **financial records, contracts, and expense reports**.  

---

## 3️⃣ How to request

1. Make a **written request** to the board or property manager  
2. Specify what you want (e.g., “all invoices over $500 from the last 12 months”)  
3. The corporation **must provide access within a reasonable time**  

> They **cannot refuse without legal reason**.  
> If they do, you can escalate to the **Condominium Authority Tribunal (CAT)**.

---

## 4️⃣ Limits

- They **may redact personal information** (other owners, staff)
- They **cannot withhold records just to hide spending**
- You **cannot demand documents unrelated to the corporation**  

---

## 5️⃣ Why this matters

- Ensures **transparency**  
- Prevents **misuse of funds**  
- Confirms **board acts in good faith**  

---

### ✅ Bottom line

> As an owner, you have the **right to see exactly what your condo fees are spent on**, including contractor invoices, reimbursements, and general expenses.  
> The board **cannot hide this**, and you can **legally request full disclosure**.

### ✅ Step 2: Rule must be **lawful in substance**
A rule can **ONLY** be made to:
- Promote **safety**
- Promote **security**
- Protect **common elements**

❌ A rule **CANNOT**:
- Take away ownership rights
- Restrict use that the **Declaration allows**
- Act like a by-law or amendment
- Create penalties or punishments

---

### ✅ Step 3: **Owners must be notified in writing**
This part is critical ⬇️

- The rule **must be sent to all owners**
- Owners get **at least 30 days’ notice**
- Owners have the right to **requisition a meeting to challenge it**

👉 **No notice = rule is NOT enforceable**

---

### ✅ Step 4: Owners’ veto right
If owners owning **15% or more of the units** requisition a meeting:
- The rule can be **voided**
- The board **cannot enforce it**

### ❌ No individual signatures are required
But this does **NOT** mean the board has free power.

Instead:
- Owners’ consent is built into the **30-day notice + veto process**
- If that process is skipped → the rule is invalid

### 🚫 NO — fines are NOT allowed in Ontario

This is **very clear law**:

> **Condo boards CANNOT issue fines or penalties**, even if the rule exists.

They may only:
- Seek **compliance**
- Charge back **actual, documented costs** incurred (e.g., legal letter, repair)

❌ They CANNOT:
- Impose flat fines
- Invent penalties
- Charge “administrative fines”
- Punish owners

Calling it a “fee” or “penalty” does **not** make it legal.

That is **legally wrong**.

A rule is **invalid** if:
- It was not properly noticed
- It exceeds the board’s authority
- It conflicts with the Declaration
- It creates a fine or punishment
- It is enforced selectively

✔ Proof of **30-day owner notice**
