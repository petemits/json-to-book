# ✅ **How to Detect a Hidden (Spy) Camera**

## **1. Physical Inspection (Most Important)**
Look carefully at common hiding spots:
- **Smoke alarms**
- **Air vents**
- **Small holes** in walls or ceilings
- **Picture frames**
- **Clocks**
- **USB chargers**
- **Lamps**
- **Mirrors** (two-way mirrors)

**Tip:** Spy cameras must have a **tiny pinhole lens** (often black and circular).

---

## **2. Use Your Phone Flashlight**
Turn off the lights and shine your phone flashlight slowly around the room.

➡️ Camera lenses reflect light like a tiny **glowing dot**.

Move the light slowly and check:
- Walls  
- Power outlets  
- Decorations  
- Electronics  

---

## **3. Use Your Phone Camera to Detect Infrared (IR)**
Many hidden cameras use IR LEDs for night vision.

### **How to test**
1. Turn off the lights.
2. Open your phone camera.
3. Point it around suspicious objects.

➡️ If you see **small glowing purple/white dots**, that may be an IR light from a hidden camera.

(Front cameras on many phones detect IR better.)

---

## **4. Use a Wi-Fi Scanner App**
Hidden Wi-Fi cameras often appear as devices on your network.

### Apps you can use:
- **Fing**
- **Network Analyzer**

Look for devices like:
- Unknown IP cameras  
- “IPCam”
- “ESP_XXXX”
- “Camera_XXXX”
- Strange MAC addresses

---

## **5. Use an RF (Radio Frequency) Detector**
If someone is transmitting video wirelessly, a cheap RF detector can help.

Price: **$15–$40** on Amazon or local electronics shops.

It can detect:
- Wireless cameras  
- Wi-Fi cameras  
- Bluetooth trackers  
- Audio bugs  

---

## **6. Check for Unusual Heat**
Spy cameras produce small heat.

Use your hand to feel for:
- Warm spots on walls  
- Warm USB chargers  
- Warm power adapters

---

## **7. Listen for Clicking / Buzzing**
Some cameras make small sounds when adjusting or powering up.

Put your ear close to vents, clocks, or small electronics.
