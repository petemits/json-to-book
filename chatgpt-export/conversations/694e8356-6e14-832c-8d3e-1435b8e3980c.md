Short answer: **Yes, a condominium corporation may try to recover a “non-registration” amount only if it reflects *actual, reasonable compliance-related costs*—but it is *not* an automatic fee, penalty, or fine.**

Here’s how this works **under Ontario law**, clearly and precisely:

---

## 1. Condos in Ontario **cannot charge fines**
Under the **Condominium Act, 1998 (Ontario)**:

- Condominium corporations **do not have the legal authority to impose fines or penalties**
- Any amount charged **must be tied to real, documented costs**
- Labels like *“non-registration fee,” “penalty,” “charge,” or “fine” **do not make it lawful**

So if a condo says *“$___ non-registration fee”* → that wording **alone is not valid**.

---

## 2. What they *can* recover instead
A condo corporation may recover **actual compliance-related costs**, such as:

- Cost of **sending compliance letters**
- **Management time** spent addressing the issue (must be reasonable)
- **Legal costs** (only if a lawyer was actually involved)
- Administrative costs **directly caused by the owner’s non-compliance**

⚠️ These costs must be:
- **Real**
- **Reasonable**
- **Itemized**
- **Causally connected** to the breach

They **cannot**:
- Charge a flat or preset “non-registration fee”
- Profit from the charge
- Enforce it without notice and opportunity to comply

---

## 3. Registration of occupants / roommates
If a declaration or rules require registration of occupants:

- Failure to register **is not automatically chargeable**
- The corporation must first:
  1. **Notify you of the breach**
  2. **Give you time to comply**
  3. **Only then** recover *actual costs incurred* to enforce compliance

If you later comply **without the corporation incurring costs**, then:
👉 **There is nothing to recover**

---

## 4. When a “non-registration fee” is unlawful
A charge is likely **illegal** if:

- It is a **fixed amount** (e.g., “$250 non-registration fee”)
- No invoices, logs, or breakdown are provided
- No lawyer or manager actually incurred costs
- No prior written notice or compliance opportunity was given
- It is applied retroactively or selectively

In those cases, it is effectively a **disguised fine**, which Ontario law does **not allow**.
