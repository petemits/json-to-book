Dear Sir/Madam,

I am writing to request your legal assistance in pursuing a civil litigation case concerning medical malpractice and professional negligence involving a finger surgery that was conducted without proper informed consent, adequate diagnostic care, or adherence to established ethical procedures. The surgery has left me with permanent pain, impaired hand function, and psychological distress.

In November 2023, I sustained a finger dislocation and presented at Trillium Hospital in Mississauga. The first attempt to relocate my finger involved twisting it around in an effort to return it to its original position. This attempt failed. The emergency doctor attending to me was not a surgeon, but a physician whose role was limited to referring me to a specialist. He referred me to a finger plastic surgeon named Dr. Paul Walker in Burlington St. Joseph Hospital, implying that most surgeries of this kind result in full finger function. However, he could not, or did not, explain the severity of my specific case or the potential complications that might arise from the surgery. Had he disclosed the risks of the injury, I would’ve declined the procedure of the surgery. 

The surgeon then strongly urged me to undergo the procedure, explaining that it would involve cutting the finger at a specific joint to place it back into its original location. I was assured that the surgery would be straightforward, pain-free, and fully successful. Based on these representations, I proceeded but at no point was I provided with a written or verbal consent form, nor was I informed of the risks, long-term outcomes, or alternative treatments.

During surgery, the surgeon remarked I should have come in earlier but still assured me of success. To address the mislocation, he cut through the whole finger to locate just one joint, a step that could have been better understood with proper X-ray assessment beforehand. My dislocated joint was at the knuckles joint, why would he cut the other joints. That's careless on the surgeon's behalf.  After the operation, he said I would regain full finger function in 6–8 months and referred me to physiotherapy. The physiotherapist discovered a hanging stitch, stating therapy could not proceed until it was removed and that no improvement was possible otherwise. Dissolving stitches should have been used. My family doctor later advised that regaining full function would require breaking the finger again. A specialist and another surgeon have since confirmed I should have been informed of the likely outcome and that the performing surgeon should have been more transparent. As you know it takes 2 to 3 months to get the specialist appointment, I have seen one surgeon twice for the post surgery recovery and I have an appointment to seek new advice from the top surgeon in plastic surgery.

Dear Dr. Walker,  

I am writing to formally notify you of my intent to pursue civil litigation arising from the surgery you performed on my finger in November 2023 at St. Joseph’s Hospital, Burlington.  

The grounds for this claim are medical malpractice, professional negligence, and lack of informed consent.  

### Basis of Complaint  
1. **Lack of Informed Consent:**  
   - At no stage was I provided with a written or verbal consent form.  
   - I was not informed of the risks, potential complications, long-term outcomes, or alternative treatments.  
   - I was assured that the procedure would be straightforward, pain-free, and result in full finger recovery.  

2. **Surgical Procedure and Aftermath:**  
   - During surgery, you cut through multiple finger joints instead of addressing the actual dislocated knuckle joint.  
   - This decision was made without adequate diagnostic measures such as MRI or complete X-ray assessment.  
   - Post-surgery, I was assured of full recovery within 6–8 months, yet I continue to experience permanent pain and impaired function.  
   - My physiotherapist found a hanging stitch, confirming therapy could not proceed until removal. Dissolving stitches should have been used.  

3. **Failure to Provide Alternatives:**  
   - No physiotherapy or non-surgical recovery options were discussed prior to surgery.  
   - Subsequent medical consultations have confirmed that surgery was not the only treatment pathway.  

4. **Impact on My Life and Livelihood:**  
   - I suffer ongoing pain, reduced hand mobility, and psychological distress.  
   - My ability to resume employment, after retraining from a previous stroke, has been derailed, leading to significant financial and career loss.  
   - The cumulative damages are estimated at over $150,000 in lost earning potential, aside from ongoing suffering.  

5. **Additional Duty of Care:**  
   - My medical history includes schizophrenia and a stroke in 2021. These documented conditions impair memory and decision-making capacity. This made it all the more imperative that you obtain clear, documented informed consent, which did not occur.  

### Legal Position  
Under Canadian medical law and ethical standards, a surgeon must:  
- Obtain informed consent with full disclosure of risks and alternatives.  
- Provide appropriate diagnostic evaluation prior to invasive surgery.  
- Refrain from performing a procedure where the expected outcome is unlikely or uncertain without full patient knowledge and agreement.  

In my case, these obligations were not met.  

### Next Steps  
I am seeking accountability and damages for the harm caused. Unless this matter is resolved amicably, I intend to proceed with formal legal action for medical malpractice and negligence.  

Please treat this as a pre-civil litigation notice. I request that you respond in writing within **30 days** of receipt to indicate your position.  

Sincerely,  
   
**Amit Puri**
